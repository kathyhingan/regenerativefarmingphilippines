Who it's for

Three paths, and the method meets you where you are.

By Kathy Hingan & Daryl Urbanski2 October 2026

Short answerRegenerative farming meets a farmer where they are, and there are three paths: the curious farmer still farming chemically and deciding, the starter who has decided and needs the first season, and the practicing steward already farming regeneratively who wants to systematize, measure, and sell on verified practice.

The three paths

Most farming advice assumes one audience and one starting point. The reality is three different farmers in three different places, and the method is built so each starts where they actually are:

The Curious Farmer: still deciding

Still farming chemically, watching input costs climb, wondering whether regenerative is viable or just another word. The honest answer lives on the Curious Farmer page: the real benefits ranked by when they arrive, the honest disadvantages nobody markets, and the arithmetic of the first season. This path starts with the definition and the numbers, because the decision is a business one.

The Starter: decided, needs the first season

Has decided to change and needs the method, the calendar, and the inputs. The Starter page sizes the first season honestly: cover the bare soil, start one compost pile, write everything down, and skip the big purchases. It covers backyard scale, smallholder scale, and the beginner mistakes that cost a year, learned the expensive way on our own farm.

The Practicing Steward: farming it, wants the system

Already farming regeneratively, mostly by instinct, and wants to systematize, measure, and possibly certify. the Practicing Steward page covers the certification landscape honestly (no universal standard exists yet, and what that means), how to prove practice without greenwashing it, and how to sell on a verified record.

The quiz tells you which path you are on. The method tells you what to do next.

Where each path starts in the method

The ten modules meet each path at a different door, and starting at the wrong door is how transitions fail:

  • Curious Farmer: module one, the definition, then module nine, the arithmetic. Decide on the numbers, not on the marketing.
  • Starter: module two, soil, then module three, compost, then module eight, the first-season plan. The first season is the path.
  • Practicing Steward: module ten, measurement, then module nine, then loop back through what you have been doing by instinct.

What the curious farmer's first season looks like

Because most people reading this page are on the curious path, it is worth spelling their season out. It is not a conversion season; it is a decision season. The farm keeps running its usual way while one field, one bed, or one backyard plot runs the regenerative way alongside it: the straw mulched instead of burned, the cover crop on the gap, the compost pile small and running, the notebook holding the four soil tests.

What that parallel season produces, and why it decides: the input numbers for both systems side by side, in your own currency, from your own ground. The regenerative half's costs fall while the conventional half's do not, and by the season's end the farmer is not deciding on an article, a quiz, or a neighbor's opinion. They are deciding on their own numbers, from their own land, which is the only decision that survives the neighbors and the hard years.

The curious path's promise, stated honestly: we cannot tell you it will work on your ground. We can tell you it works on ours, and the first season's parallel test is how you find out whether it does on yours, for the cost of a packet of seed and the straw you were already burning.

What every path shares

Whatever the entry point, three things are the same for every farmer, and they are the reason the method is one method rather than three:

  • The five practices. Living roots, covered soil, minimal disturbance, diversity, feeding the biology. Every path leads here, and no path skips any of them.
  • The notebook. The soil tests and the costs, written down with dates. The curious farmer uses it to decide; the starter uses it to stay; the steward uses it to sell. Same notebook, different job.
  • The seasons. Wet and dry, not spring and fall. Every path learns the tropical calendar, because timing is where imported advice fails and where this method is different.

How to know which path you are on

Three questions, asked in order, and they place you exactly:

  1. Are you still using chemical inputs as the main tool? If yes, and you are wondering whether any of this is viable, you are a curious farmer. Your path starts with the definition and the arithmetic, because the decision is a business decision.
  2. Have you decided to change, and are you planning the first season? Then you are a starter. Your path is the first three modules and the first-season plan, sized to one field or one bed.
  3. Are you already farming regeneratively, and is the next step systematizing or selling? Then you are a practicing steward. Your path starts with the measurement module and the certification landscape.

The Find Your Path quiz asks the same questions visually, five steps, and it tells you which module to start with based on what you grow and what is costing you the most. Same result as the three questions above, delivered in twenty seconds.

Why three paths instead of one

Because the advice a curious farmer needs is different from the advice a steward needs, and a single page serving both serves neither. The curious farmer needs honesty about costs and a decision framework; the starter needs a season sized to their reality; the steward needs the certification landscape and the selling path. One page covering all three would be generic, and generic is what the imported advice already is.

The three-path structure is also how the transition actually happens on the ground: nobody goes from chemical farming to a verified regenerative record in one season. The stages are real, and the method treats them as real instead of pretending every reader is in the same place.

The farm the paths are taught from

Every path on this page leads through the same farm: Viking Farms PH in Calamba, Laguna, three generations on the same land. The grandfather and grandmother farmed it, the father farms it now and leads the barangay Farmers Association, and the method it teaches was farmed there first, in both seasons. The dragonfruit that pays, the munggo on the paddies' edges, the straw that became mulch instead of smoke: all of it is on that ground, and all of it is what the paths are built from.

What the starter's first season produces

The starter's season is the build season, and its outputs are concrete: a covered field or bed with living roots in it through the season, one compost pile that has been turned and finished and is ready for the next, a biofertilizer brew that worked or a note on why it did not, and a notebook with three seasons' worth of soil tests and costs in it. None of those outputs is a harvest, and all of them are the system that makes every harvest after possible.

The honest warning that goes with it: the starter's season has flat numbers where the curious farmer's parallel test had a comparison. Both the yields and the weeds will look wrong at points, and the notebook is what separates a normal first season from a broken one. The farmers who reach the steward stage are the ones who kept the notebook through it.

What the steward's system produces

The steward's stage produces the things a farm can sell on and hand over: a soil record with seasons of trend in it, input numbers that prove the economics, a calendar the next generation or a hired hand can follow, and a story with the hard parts in it. That record is what premium buyers ask for, what a cooperative carries, and what any future certification will ask to see. It is also the stage where the work stops being one farmer's instinct and becomes the farm's property, which is the only version of a method that survives its founder.

Frequently asked questions

Which path am I on?

Still using chemical inputs and unsure: you are a curious farmer. Decided and needing the first season: a starter. Already farming regeneratively and wanting to systematize: a practicing steward. The Find Your Path quiz tells you the same thing from your answers about how you farm today.

Can I move between paths?

Everyone does. A curious farmer decides and becomes a starter; a starter's first season turns into a steward's system. The paths are stages, not boxes, and the method is built to be walked in that order.

I am not a farmer yet. Do these paths still apply?

Yes, starting at the curious or starter path. The method works at garden and container scale, and the beginner path covers starting on a backyard bed or a friend's land, which is where most farmers actually start before they have a farm of their own.

Do all three paths use the same course?

Yes, entered at different modules. The course is one method in ten modules; the paths are the three orders a farmer works through it, depending on where their farm is when they begin.
