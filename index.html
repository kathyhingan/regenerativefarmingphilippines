Our story

The farm our family kept farming.

Since the beginning

Our grandparents farmed this land. Our father still does.

They planted rice and sugarcane, and vegetables alongside it: tomatoes, squash, eggplants, calamansi, and whatever else the season allowed. That is not a brand story we invented. It is simply what our family has always done.

When our grandmother passed away, our father carried it on. He kept the land working. Around us, a lot of the land turned industrial, and the farms went quiet. Ours did not.

Today he leads the Farmers Association in our barangay, working with the city of Calamba to keep people interested in farming, to keep them learning, and to keep them active on their own land.

That is who is behind it: not a company that discovered farming, but Viking Farms PH, a family that never left the land, now teaching the method that keeps a farm worth keeping.

Before you ask

Common questions, answered plainly.

What is regenerative farming?

Regenerative farming is a way of farming that rebuilds soil health instead of depleting it. It works through a small set of practices: keeping living roots in the soil, keeping the soil covered, disturbing the soil as little as possible, growing a diversity of crops, and feeding the soil biology rather than only feeding the plant. The result is lower input costs, soil that holds water better, and a farm that keeps producing.

Can this work on a small farm in the Philippines?

Yes, and it is often easier on a small farm than a large one. The method is built around using what the farm already produces, rice straw, manure, crop residues and on-farm seed, which lowers cost rather than raising it. The course is written for tropical wet and dry seasons rather than temperate spring and fall, because timing is where most imported advice fails.

Who is teaching this?

A farming family from Calamba, Laguna. The grandparents farmed the land, the father farms it today, and he leads the barangay Farmers Association working with the city to keep farming alive and farmers educated. The brand is that family teaching what it practices.

No. Joining the movement is free and puts you on the list for the first guides. When they are ready, the first buyers come in at a Founding Farmer rate in exchange for honest feedback from their own land.
